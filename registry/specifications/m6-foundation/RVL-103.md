# RVL-103 — behaviour specification

Wave: `waves/m6-foundation.json`. Method: source-informed reimplementation.
Rights: the upstream project's repository root carries an MIT licence file whose
copyright line names the same author as RVL-199's; see
`provenance/m6-foundation/RVL-103.json` for the pinned revision and digest.

Confidence markers: **read**, **inferred**, **unknown**.

---

## 1. What this entry is

An extension of RVL-199, written by the same author, that adds one tier above
the top of that entry's crop progression together with the materials, fuels,
foods, tools, fluids and ores that tier implies. It is a leaf: nothing else in
the wave depends on it, and it depends on RVL-199 and RVL-162 (**read** — it
declares both and consumes RVL-199's public extension interface).

It is small in source and large in data: about 35 source files against about 480
data and asset files (**read**). As with RVL-199, **the catalogue is data and
this document does not write it out as prose**. What follows specifies the
systems; §12 makes the split explicit.

Everything it adds enters through RVL-199's published extension interface. It
registers no mechanic of its own that RVL-199 does not already define a shape
for, with two exceptions: the fuel rule (§6) and the food rule (§7).

---

## 2. How it extends the entry it builds on

It publishes one plugin class against RVL-199's marked-plugin mechanism
(**read**). In the configuration callback it does two things:

1. declares the namespace its content belongs to; and
2. switches off all three of RVL-199's dynamic seed-recipe generators for that
   namespace — the crafting, infusion and reprocessing generators alike.

The second is load-bearing. RVL-199 generates seed recipes for every registered
crop unless told not to; this entry supplies its own hand-written recipes for
every crop it registers instead (**read** — the recipe files are present in its
own data tree). An implementer that keeps the generators on for this namespace
gets duplicate recipes, not missing ones.

In the crop callbacks it registers one tier and six crops in the first pass, and
in the post-registration pass fills in the parts of both that refer to
registrations the first pass could not yet see. This is exactly the paired shape
RVL-199 §2 describes and is the clearest evidence that the pairing exists for
the reason that document infers (**inferred**).

---

## 3. The tier it adds

One crop tier, ordinal 6, one rank above the highest RVL-199 defines (**read**).
The post-registration pass gives it four things:

- a farmland block (§4),
- an essence item (§5),
- whether it is fertilizable, taken from configuration rather than fixed
  (**read** — this is the only tier in either entry whose fertilizable flag is
  configurable, and it defaults to off), and
- **no secondary seed drop**: the flag RVL-199 §3.4 describes is set false.

The tier also carries a colour and a text style for display. Both are catalogue.

The fertilizable value is read once, at post-registration time, into the tier
(**read**). A configuration change after that point does not reach the tier
without a reload of the registry. This is a consequence of the ordering, not a
stated intent (**inferred**).

---

## 4. The farmland block

One block per tier, a copy of the vanilla farmland block with three behavioural
differences (**read**):

- **Falling on it never converts it back to dirt.** The vanilla block's trample
  behaviour is replaced with plain fall damage, calculated at the vanilla
  multiplier and attributed to the vanilla fall damage source. The block is
  unaffected.
- **Moisture never decays to a dry state that stops growth prematurely**: it is
  fertile whenever its moisture is above zero.
- **Its random tick is unconditional.** Vanilla's farmland dries out and then
  reverts to dirt; this one only moves moisture. If there is no water adjacent
  and no rain above, moisture drops by one and stops at zero; otherwise moisture
  is set straight to its maximum of seven rather than incremented.

It reports the tier it belongs to, and tints itself with that tier's colour
through a colour interface RVL-162 publishes.

**inferred**: the point of the block is that a crop of this tier will only grow
on it, which is RVL-199's effective-farmland rule (§3.3 there) applied to a tier
whose own block is this one. Nothing in this entry restates that rule.

---

## 5. The essence item and farmland conversion

One item per tier. Used on a block, it converts that block to the tier's
farmland through RVL-199's conversion rule (§3.6 there), and does nothing else.

The conversion is gated on a feature flag **owned by RVL-199, not by this
entry** (**read**). If that flag is off the use is refused before the conversion
is attempted, and the interaction passes through to whatever would otherwise
handle it rather than being consumed. An implementer that owns the flag locally
gets the wrong answer when the base entry's configuration turns conversion off
globally.

---

## 6. Fuels

Two families of fuel item, one loose and one in block form, six ordinal tiers
each (**read**). Each declares a burn duration directly rather than through the
data-driven fuel registry, so the value is code and not data.

The rule the numbers describe:

- the loose form's lowest tier burns for 2400 ticks, and each tier above burns
  for exactly twice the tier below, up to 76800 at tier 6;
- the block form of a tier burns for exactly nine times its loose form.

Both statements hold across all six tiers with no exception (**read** — checked
against every value). Nine is the crafting ratio between the two forms, so the
block form is worth exactly what its contents are worth, and an implementer
should derive the block values rather than carry a second table.

---

## 7. Foods

Six tiers of food item, one per tier of the combined progression (**read**).
Each is always edible regardless of hunger, always draws with an enchantment
glint, and carries nutrition and saturation values that rise with the tier.

Eating one, **on the server side only**, applies a list of status effects. The
lists are strictly cumulative: each tier applies every effect the tier below
applies, plus one more. Six tiers, six effects, so the top tier applies all six
and the bottom applies one.

Each effect is applied at amplifier index 1 — the second level — and its
duration is **added to whatever duration the eater already has remaining for
that effect** rather than replacing it (**read**). Eating repeatedly therefore
extends rather than refreshes, without bound. The per-application duration is a
configured number of seconds converted to ticks.

### 7.1 A defect to diverge from

The hover text that lists a food's effects is built on first request and cached
in the item for the life of the process (**read**). It reads the configured
duration at that moment. Because the configuration is a startup-type
configuration this rarely misleads, but a reload that changes the duration
leaves every already-inspected food describing the old one, and the two food
items differ in what they claim depending on which was inspected first.

A counterpart should compute the text on each request and not cache a value
derived from configuration. This is the same class of defect as RVL-199 §5's
cached display name and should be recorded as a deliberate divergence in the
same way.

## 8. Drops from two bosses

One handler on the living-drops event, server side (**read**):

1. If the mob-drops game rule is off, it adds nothing. This check comes first
   and covers both drops.
2. A wither-class boss drops one of a soul item, with a configured probability
   defaulting to 0.35.
3. A dragon-class boss drops a configured count of a scale item, defaulting to
   8, and drops nothing when that count is zero.

Both are added to the event's drop list as world items positioned at the dying
entity, not merged into a loot table (**read**), so a loot-table replacement
does not remove them.

### 8.1 A second defect to diverge from

The probability draw uses a **process-global random source, not the level's**
(**read**). That makes the drop unreproducible from a world seed and makes it
draw from a source shared with anything else in the process. A counterpart
should draw from the level's own random source. Record the divergence.

---

## 9. Ore generation

Four biome modifiers, one per (material, dimension) pair over two materials and
two non-overworld dimensions (**read**). Each is a data-declared modifier
carrying a biome set and one placed feature, and each adds its feature to the
underground-ores decoration step during the add phase only.

Two properties matter (**read**):

- the modifier consults **its own configuration flag at modify time**, so
  turning generation off takes effect on the next world load without editing
  the data files; and
- it applies only to biomes in its own declared set, which is data.

The features themselves, their placement, and the biome sets are data.

---

## 10. Molten fluids

Nine fluid pairs — a still and a flowing form each — with one fluid type per
pair (**read**). Every type carries the same physical properties: density 2000,
viscosity 10000, temperature 1000, and the vanilla lava bucket sounds for fill
and empty. They are lava-like by construction; nothing distinguishes them from
one another except their identity and their colour.

Each has a bucket item, and every bucket is registered against the fluid-handler
capability for items through a standard single-fluid bucket handler.

**unknown**: whether anything consumes these fluids within this entry. The
recipes that would say so were not read.

---

## 11. The augmentable tool

One tool type, a combined pickaxe/shovel/axe, in seven materials (**read**). It
implements RVL-199's upgradeable-item interface and declares four of that
entry's upgrade target kinds: the generic tool kind plus the three specific
kinds its own shape covers.

Its behaviour is entirely delegation (**read**): on use against a block, use in
the air, use on an entity, hitting an entity, breaking a block, and each
inventory tick, it walks its installed upgrades and calls the corresponding
upgrade hook. For the four hooks that return a result, **any** upgrade returning
success makes the whole interaction succeed; the tool's own behaviour runs only
when none did. Breaking a block is the exception: the tool's own break behaviour
runs first and unconditionally.

A material with zero durability makes the item unbreakable, which is how the
top material is expressed without a special case (**read**). Upgrade slot counts
are catalogue: six materials get one and the seventh gets two.

---

## 12. Configuration

One startup-type configuration file, eight values in two groups (**read**):
the soul drop chance (0.35, range 0–1), the scale drop count (8, range 0–64),
whether the new tier's crops are fertilizable (false), the food effect duration
in seconds (180, minimum 1), and four independent booleans for the four ore
generations (all true).

Five of these are also exposed as feature flags through RVL-162's flag
mechanism, and one further flag is **imported from RVL-199** rather than
declared here (§5).

---

## 13. Content is data, not specification

Six crops, one tier, roughly thirty blocks, sixty items, nine fluids, and
several hundred recipe, tag, loot and language files. None of it is written out
here for the reason RVL-199 §8 gives: reproducing a catalogue through prose is
the transliteration failure the clean-room process exists to prevent.

Four of the six crops are conditional on a third-party mod being installed, and
each is enabled only when its own required mod is present. **In a non-production
environment that gate is bypassed entirely and all crops are enabled** (**read**)
— a development affordance that a counterpart should either implement
deliberately or not implement at all, but should not implement by accident.

---

## 14. What this specification does not specify

- **Everything in §13.**
- **What the fluids are for** (§10), marked unknown.
- **Recipes, tags, loot tables and language files.** Present in bulk, unread
  beyond their directory shape.
- **The client surface**: fluid model registration and block tint registration
  are named and their mechanisms unread.
- **The optional integrations.** Two of the three exist only as fully
  commented-out files carrying an obsolete API and contribute no behaviour at
  the pinned revision (**read**); the third is a hover-text provider whose only
  content is the tier line of §4.
- **Creative-menu grouping.** One group, ordered by hand, gated per entry on the
  same enablement as §13. Presentation, not behaviour.
- **Coverage.** Read in full: the plugin, the tier, the crops' enablement rule,
  the farmland block, the essence item, both fuel families, the food tiers and
  their effect rule, the drop handler, all four biome modifiers, the fluid types,
  the tool, and the configuration. Read in part: the block and item declarations,
  for the values §6 and §11 quote. Everything else: listed, not read.
