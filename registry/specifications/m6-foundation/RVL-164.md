# RVL-164 — behaviour specification

Wave: `waves/m6-foundation.json` (foundation-method pilot). Method: source-informed
reimplementation. Rights: the upstream project's repository root carries an MIT
licence file; see `provenance/m6-foundation/RVL-164.json` for the pinned artifact
and digest.

Confidence markers: **read**, **inferred**, **unknown**.

---

## 1. What this entry is

A single hand-held tool item that measures the axis-aligned region between two
block positions a player selects, and a client-side drawing of that region with
its dimensions (**read**). It registers one item and nothing else that changes
world state (**read**). Everything it draws is client presentation; the only
persistent effect is the item's own registration.

It is tiny in source and small in data (about twenty source files, most of them
platform and client-render plumbing) (**read**).

---

## 2. The selection interaction

Using the tool on a block selects a position (**read**). The interaction is a
two-step toggle per holder:

1. The first use records a start position.
2. The second use records an end position and completes a region spanning the
   two.
3. A completed region is what the client draws; a further use begins a new
   selection (**inferred** — the source keeps one active region per holder and
   replaces it, rather than accumulating; the exact reset trigger beyond "a new
   first click" was not traced in full).

The interaction succeeds and is consumed when it selects a position; otherwise it
does nothing and passes through to default handling (**read**).

Selection is a per-player, transient, client-facing concern. It is not written to
the save and does not survive a session (**inferred** — the region lives in a
client-side handler keyed by holder, with no persistence path read).

---

## 3. The region and its measurements

Given two block positions, the region is the smallest axis-aligned box that
contains both whole blocks (**read**). Concretely:

- The low corner is the per-axis minimum of the two positions.
- The high corner is the per-axis maximum, plus one on each axis, because a block
  occupies a unit of space and the box must enclose the block at the high corner
  rather than stop at its near face (**read** — the `+1` on each axis is present
  and load-bearing; without it a same-block selection has zero size instead of a
  one-cube size).

From that box the tool reports (**read** / **inferred**):

- **Per-axis lengths**: the width, height and depth of the box, each a whole
  number of blocks (**read**).
- **Straight-line distance**: the Euclidean distance across the region
  (**inferred** — the box diagonal is the natural straight-line measure and the
  render path computes distances with the standard metric; whether the tool
  surfaces the diagonal specifically, or the per-axis spans only, was not fully
  determined). A counterpart should expose both the per-axis spans and a
  Euclidean magnitude and let the presentation choose.
- **Block-count distance**: the sum of the per-axis spans (the taxicab metric),
  which is the count a block-builder cares about (**inferred** — this is the
  useful measure for the tool's purpose; it is offered as a deliberate, clearly
  useful addition rather than a claim about the original surfacing it).

A same-position selection yields a one-by-one-by-one region (**read** — a
consequence of the `+1` rule).

---

## 4. Configuration and presentation

A line colour and a text colour are configurable, and the drawing is skipped
beyond a fixed viewing distance from the region's centre (**read** — a cull at a
constant range). Colours, fonts, the on-screen text, and the draw itself are
client presentation and are **not** part of this specification: a counterpart
draws through the host's own client surface (**read**).

---

## 5. What a counterpart implements

The portable, host-independent core is the region geometry of §3: forming the
enclosing box from two integer positions with the per-axis `+1`, and deriving the
per-axis spans, the Euclidean magnitude, and the taxicab sum. That geometry is
pure arithmetic and is what this counterpart provides. The item registration is a
host registry concern; selection state and all drawing are client concerns.

---

## 6. What this specification does not specify

- **The client drawing** (§4): line/text rendering, colours, fonts, the distance
  cull constant, and the on-screen dimension labels. Named, mechanisms unread
  beyond the cull's existence.
- **The exact selection-reset state machine** (§2), marked inferred: whether a
  third click resets or extends, and any keybind or sneak modifier, were not
  traced.
- **Whether the tool surfaces the diagonal, the per-axis spans, or both** (§3),
  marked inferred.
- **Platform plumbing**: registration factory, services, and login/handler wiring
  — host concerns, listed not specified.
- **Coverage.** Read in full: the item's use-on interaction and its two-step
  selection, and the region-box construction including the per-axis `+1`. Read in
  part: the client render path, for the distance cull and the per-axis length
  reads §3 quotes. Everything else (config screens, platform services, datagen):
  listed, not read.
